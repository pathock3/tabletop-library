# DECISIONS — Fossil Frenzy

Unclear rules and judgment calls, grouped by topic. The owner can overrule any of them; most are single values in the data block (`GAME_DATA` in `index.html`) and every one has a test. The in-game *How to play* panel lists the ones that matter at the table.

## Where the rules come from
1. **One rule set.** `docs/design-summary.md` (Patrick's redesign: no pieces, no movement, no delivery; a turn is peek-and-flip or bank) plus the owner's later feedback *is* the game. `rulebook.md` is the original physical game, kept as history; its movement and delivery rules are **no longer implemented** (they were removed on 2026-10-18 at the owner's request: "don't have two different rule sets"). The removed code and its tests are in git history, last present at commit `369ea0b`.
2. **Where sources disagree, the owner's latest word wins:** the 2026-10-18 feedback replaced the summary's "1 point per set" with "the number on the fossil is the points; bank ONE set"; and asked for Pass & Play (up to 4) and a five-level Campaign. The summary's tiered-AI memory *decay* and "overconfidence" are not implemented (memory is the task's "remembers with a set probability").
3. **The designer is credited once**, at the top of the Tabletop Library, as Patrick Blim-Hockberger (changed surname), not per game. The designer line in `rulebook.md` (otherwise a verbatim copy of the original PDF) was updated to the new surname at the owner's request.

## Board and setup
4. **A hexagon of 31 spaces** (radius 3 = 37 spaces, minus the six corners), one fossil on each. The count follows from the tile set (31) and the summary ("31 spaces, exactly one tile per space"). Board radius, removed spaces and the fossil list are data; the engine refuses a fossil count that does not match the space count.
5. **Who starts:** everyone rolls a die; a duplicate roll is re-rolled by whoever rolled it second; highest goes first; play continues clockwise (seat order). The roll is shown on a "Who goes first?" screen, one tap.

## Peek, flip, match
6. **First tap = a private peek at any fossil; second tap = a public flip of another.** The peek shows on your screen (honour system on a shared device: tap the peeked fossil again to hide it). A peek does not use up the turn: you may bank instead, or just end the turn (the peek stays private).
7. **Unmatched fossils flip back at the end of the turn** that revealed them ("memory game"; the summary agrees). No fossils stay face up on the board.
8. **A match is the fossil you peeked at plus the one you flipped** (same number). Pairs for numbers; a set of three for the gems — two gems never match. One peek and one match attempt per turn.
9. **Gems:** peek a gem and flip a gem → you *may* tap your peeked gem to reveal it, then flip a third fossil; a gem makes the set, otherwise everything flips back. Declining (End turn) keeps your gem private. (The rulebook's other order — flip the extra fossil first — was part of the removed rule set.)
10. **The UI takes the obvious option for you:** when your peek and the flipped fossil are a pair they are taken at once; revealing your own gem is a deliberate tap because it shows information.
11. **A match stays face up on the board until the turn ends** (yours or an opponent's), instead of vanishing, so players can see what happened; the matched fossils join the player's held sets.

## Banking and scoring
12. **Bank = take ONE held set of your choice and score the number on its fossil** (a gem set scores 12), then one d6 chain: each 6 adds +1 and rolls again (kept from the design summary; `bonusDieSides: 0` removes it). Banking takes the whole turn. The other sets stay in front of you; sets never banked score nothing. Values are data.
13. **The game ends the instant the last fossil is matched.** Tie-breaks (from the summary): highest score, then most sets popped, then whoever popped the final pair, then a shared win.

## Campaign
14. **Five levels — Beginner, Novice, Normal, Challenging, Master — shown by name only (no percentages).** All five are visible; only Beginner is open at first; the other four are greyed out with a lock. You play one opponent per level.
15. **You defeat a level by winning outright.** A shared win (every tie-break level) is a draw and does not unlock anything; a loss does not either. Beating a level opens exactly the next one. Beaten levels can be replayed. Beating Master completes the campaign.
16. **Levels are opponent profiles** (superseded the memory-plus-banking-habit levels): each level is a score from 0 to 10 for each of eight named skills in the data block. See 55–63 and `docs/AI.md`.
17. **Progress is stored in the browser** (`localStorage`) per device. `?unlock` on the page's address opens every level for that visit, for testing; it saves nothing.
18. **Pass & Play is humans only (2–4)**, exempt from the campaign. The campaign is you against one opponent (so a campaign game has 2 players).
19. The campaign ladder is **monotone**, each level beating the one below it (REPORT.md, Table 6), with bigger steps at the top. The owner chose these levels; the `memory` values are the lever for making a level gentler or harder.

## Bots
20. **Memory** (rewritten, see 56): a fact fades with every turn that passes; recalling it exactly has probability `memory^age`; a slip taps a random *other* spot.
21. **Information discipline:** bots are given `Engine.publicView` only (no hidden fossil values); a test asserts the view contains none.
22. **Strategy** (rewritten, see 55): one decision engine for every opponent; what differs between opponents is a profile of abilities.
23. Opponents do not model what the *other player* privately knows (they cannot see your peeks) — listed as a possible next step in `docs/AI.md`.

## UI (the owner's "Tabletop Test")
24. **Tap the table:** fossils and held sets are the controls; no verb buttons for game actions. The small **End turn** / **Continue** button is the only pacing control, and nothing advances on a timer. An opponent's whole turn is played at once and stays on the board until the next tap.
25. **An opponent's turn shows its work:** its peek is a **dashed ring** on a face-down fossil (number hidden), its flip a **solid ring** on a face-up one; a bot that does not bank always reveals a space. Ring colour is not an accessibility gap, since only the current player's flips are ever shown.
26. **Prompts above the board** show until a device has finished one game, then go quiet (Auto / On / Off in the help panel) — a compromise between "no instructional narration" and friends who have never seen the game.
27. **End screen:** each player's banked fossils laid out with their score (a "+n" for bonus points), unbanked sets dimmed and struck through, the winner marked — not a stats table. The tiebreak reason stays as one line because the board cannot show it.
28. **Save and resume:** the game saves to the browser after every action (any phase, mid-peek, with an opponent's turn showing) and resumes automatically on load; the random numbers' position is saved, so a resumed game plays out exactly as it would have. A save made under different rules, from an older version, or damaged is cleared with a quiet note rather than resumed wrongly. A finished game or starting over clears it. Per device and browser.
29. A **library link** (‹ Tabletop Library) appears on the home screen when the game is served over http(s); opened as a standalone file there is nothing to link to.
33. **Who goes first (playtest 2):** no screen for it. The engine still rolls a die per seat at the deal (so it is random and testable), but the player never sees the roll; a **turn card** over the board names whose turn it is — at the start of every game, and before every turn in Pass & Play (it doubles as the hand-the-device-over moment). One tap on the card starts the turn. In the campaign only the start of the game shows it, since your turns follow the opponent's tap.
34. **Score is the loudest thing outside the board:** each player's points are shown as a large number in their own box along the top, the player whose turn it is lit up in their colour; a score pops briefly when it changes. Held sets are a smaller line under it.
35. **An opponent's turn is animated, one move at a time** (pace numbers are in the data block, `pace`): its peek first — the fossil lifts and tilts, a dashed ring pulses and an eye appears, the number never shown — then, after a beat, the flip, and so on. Its points and sets change only when the move that earns them appears. Nothing else moves by itself; a tap anywhere skips to the end of its turn, and then "Continue" hands the turn back. With the device's reduced-motion setting on, its whole turn appears at once.
36. **Two gems → a third tap is consent:** after you peek a gem and flip a gem, tapping any other fossil reveals your peeked gem and flips that third fossil in one go. Tapping your own peeked fossil no longer reveals it (it only hides or shows your private look), so it cannot be revealed by accident.
37. **Banking ends the turn** the moment you tap the set. What was banked (and the bonus rolls) is shown on the next turn card, or in the note while the opponent starts its turn.
38. **In-game menu:** one small ☰ button replaces the ? and ↺ buttons. It holds How to play, Retry, Back to the start and Resume. Retry and Back ask first (in the menu, not a pop-up); Retry then deals a new game at once with the same players or the same campaign level — no picking.
39. **No Continue (playtest 3):** an opponent's finished turn stays on the board (its dashed peek ring, its flip) until you tap a fossil; that tap ends its turn and *is* the first tap of yours (a peek). A banked turn needs no tap either way. Pass & Play still opens each turn with the turn card, since the device changes hands.
40. **Opponent animation is faster:** its peek appears the moment its turn starts, the flip about 0.65 s later (numbers in the data block's `pace`). A tap during it skips to the end of its turn without acting; the next tap acts.
41. **Held sets live only in the scoreboard** (no side panel): a small tile per set under each player's score. The player about to act has their sets outlined — tapping one banks it (and ends the turn). While an opponent's turn is still showing, your sets are already tappable. The one other button on the table is *End turn*, bottom right, shown only on your own turn.
42. **When an opponent banks**, a banner over the board says "<name> banked a 7", the points it scored (with the bonus rolls) and "Score now N". Taps pass through it to the map. When *you* bank, a line above the board says what you banked (and your score ticks up at the top).
43. **Home screen:** Campaign is listed first, over a picture of the real board whose tiles flip over by themselves (decoration only, shuffled each visit; still when the device asks for reduced motion). The Campaign button shows five pips for how many opponents are beaten — no percentages. The campaign screen has no explanatory line; the locks say it.
44. **Home demo is a real game (playtest 4):** instead of decoration, the home screen runs a whole game on the real engine between two opponents (blue and red, the Normal level's play), shown exactly as in play: dashed ring in the player's colour on a peek, solid ring on a flip, matched tiles vanish at the end of the turn, misses flip back, small scores underneath. When every fossil is gone the empty board waits (`homeDemo.restart`) and a new game starts. No stray ring. Timings are in the data block. It stays still when the device asks for reduced motion.
45. **The home screen is one screen** — it is laid out to the viewport and never scrolls (iPad both ways and phones); on very short screens the small print is dropped.
46. **My own peeked tile** no longer has a second, brown dashed outline: the only dashes are the coloured peek ring.
47. **Held sets are buttons:** large raised tiles; the ones you can bank right now glow green (gently pulsing) and carry a "Tap to bank" label, so it is clear that banking is an option each turn. Nothing is written when you hold no sets ("no sets" is gone), and there is no "thinking…" message while an opponent starts its turn.
48. **The opponent's bank banner is a button:** a tap on it, or anywhere else that isn't a control, clears it and starts your turn; a tap on a fossil also clears it and is your first peek.
49. **End of game offers two real buttons:** the main one (Play again / Try again, or Next: <level> with Play <level> again under it) and a full-size **Main menu** button. The old small "Levels" / "Change players" link is gone — from the main menu you reach Campaign or Pass & Play.
50. **End screen (playtest 5):** after a *win* in the campaign the only choices are the next level (when there is one) and Main menu — no replay. After a loss or draw: Try again and Main menu. Pass & Play: Play again and Main menu. Each player shows two labelled rows — **Banked** and **Matched, not banked** — with no "+" figures; the big score still includes the bonus-die points, which are no longer itemised here (they are shown when the bank happens).
51. **Unlocking is celebrated:** a big amber card ("Novice unlocked!", or "Campaign complete!") with confetti sits above the results, instead of a line of small print. Confetti is off when the device asks for reduced motion.
52. **Settings (new):** a Settings panel, reached from a small button on the home screen and from the in-game ☰ menu. It holds the prompts choice (Auto / On / Off, moved here from How to play) and **Unlock everything**. Unlock everything first shows a warning — the recommended way to play is the whole campaign, but you are welcome to unlock everything — and only then opens every campaign level. It is remembered on the device, is not the same as winning (no level is marked beaten, no progress recorded), and can be switched back. `?unlock` in the address still does the same for one visit without saving anything.
53. **Fossil values changed (playtest 6):** the 10 and 11 became a **9 and a 10**, and the **gems are worth 12** (was 16), so that two or three big fossils no longer settle a game. Only the data block changed (plus the help text, which now reads the gem value from it). The 1,000-game simulations were re-run (REPORT.md): scores fell and bunched (winning margin 6.6 → 5.2, best-to-worst gap 16.0 → 13.5), but the early leader's edge did not fall (46.1% vs 42.7% at half-time, within noise) — it comes from tempo, not from the size of the big fossils. `rulebook.md` still lists the original values; it is history.
54. **Home demo, revised (playtest 6):** each turn flips two tiles face up in the player's colour (no dashed ring) and leaves them up. They come down only when the other colour flips its first tile: a miss flips face down, a match vanishes. So the board reads blue pair → red tile → red pair → blue tile…, with no blank in between. Decoration only: the demo shows the first tile's number, which a real peek would not.
55. **One engine, many opponents (the AI work).** Every opponent — tutorial, the five levels, the perfect player — is the same decision engine in the `bots` script plus a *profile* (`GAME_DATA.ai`). The perfect player (`expert`) has every ability at 1; easier opponents have some lowered: **memory** (0.8–0.99, never perfect), **valueSense** (does it see that a 9 is worth more than a 2?), **leadSense** (plays for the win vs for points), **endSense** (how well it knows how long the game has left), **setsSense** (does it track which sets are still out there?), plus rule-of-thumb banking habits (`bankAt`, `endgameTiles`) for the easiest levels. A `random` profile exists for a future tutorial opponent. The design, the findings and the ideas not yet built are in `docs/AI.md`.
56. **Memory fades per turn, and a slip is a confident mistake** — and the bot may be **overconfident** about its memory (the `overconfidence` skill): a realistic bot explores instead of trusting a half-remembered pair, an overconfident one goes for it and sometimes taps the wrong spot. Each turn that passes (anybody's), the chance a remembered fossil is still recalled exactly is `memory`; when an opponent acts on a memory it taps the right spot with probability `memory^age` and otherwise taps a random *other* spot (so it reveals something it did not mean to). What it saw this turn is exact. A 5% floor (`ai.tuning.recallFloor`) keeps even a very forgetful opponent from being stuck for ever. Opponents are never given perfect memory in the campaign — the owner finds an occasional mistake more fun.
57. **How the perfect AI chooses.** Each turn it weighs *bank*, *take a set it can make*, *explore* (peek an unseen fossil and flip another) and *gamble on a third gem*, each scored by the position it leads to: banked points plus the worth of held sets and known pairs (limited by how many turns it expects to have left), minus the opponent's — turned into a chance of winning. The expected number of turns left is fitted to simulated games (about 2.5 turns per set still to be made). It plays from the public view and its own peeks only; a test changes the hidden fossils behind its back and checks its decision does not change.
58. **Things tried and dropped (so nobody re-tries them blind).** (a) *Flipping only already-public fossils* (to give nothing away) — worse than flipping a new one. (b) An explicit push for the leader to end the game and the trailer to stall — worse (the knob `endBias` stays at 0). (c) Searching ahead (imagining the rest of the game for each option) — no better than the rule-based planner over 300 games; removed. Findings and numbers: `docs/AI.md` and REPORT.md.
59. **Opponent save format:** an opponent saves its facts (with when it learned each), which sets are gone, its profile and its random-number position. A game saved by an earlier version (old bot format) is cleared with the usual quiet note.
60. **Playtest feedback on the AI (owner):** (a) *banking early is the default move* — the perfect AI banks a good set early (a nudge that fades as the game goes on; low sets earn less of it, so a 2 or 3 may be left); it costs no strength in simulation. (b) *Two gems found:* the naive default is to go for the third (revealing both positions); the smarter AI doesn't unless it knows where the third is — the new skill `gemSense`. (c) *Overconfidence* is a skill (above). (d) A per-move **mistake rate** (`mistakes`) exists as a gentle dial alongside memory and the other skills — the owner wants all the levers kept, not a mistake rate alone. (e) **Skills are scored 0–10** in the data block (memory and the mistake rate stay natural numbers). (f) The easiest level is made deliberately easy (memory 0.70, overconfident, 20% mistakes, banks at 2 sets, always goes for the third gem) so a new player feels good; it is to be tuned from play.
61. **Every aspect of an opponent is a named skill scored 0–10** (`GAME_DATA.skills`): Memory, Self-awareness (the opposite of overconfidence), Value sense, Lead sense, Endgame sense, Set tracking, Gem sense, Focus (the opposite of a random-move rate). Memory 0–10 maps to the chance a memory survives a turn (50% → 100%) and Focus 0–10 to the chance of a random move (50% → 0%); the rule-of-thumb banking habits are no longer separate knobs — a very low Endgame sense banks by rule of thumb (`ai.tuning.endHabits`). The data block is the single place to change a level.
62. **The ladder was set for a smooth climb by simulation.** Beginner (deliberately easy) and Master (hard but beatable) are fixed first; the levels between sit along the line joining them at positions chosen so each beats the one below by about the same margin (67 / 72 / 73 / 73%). Calibrated bot against bot; the owner will tune from play.
63. **A memory slip is a near miss half the time** (`ai.tuning.nearMiss`), and an opponent that slipped mutters something on its score box (`ai.slipSays`) until you act — the first of the "make it feel alive" ideas. Play styles are saved for later.

## Simulation (REPORT.md)
30. Fixed definitions: seeds are `seed·100000 + game number`; "early leader" = the top scorer after 25 / 50 / 75 % of the game's turns, a tied lead sharing its credit; a shared win counts as a fractional win; head-to-head cells alternate who sits first. The "human proxy" is a bot of a given memory banking promptly — an ordering of difficulty, not a promise of a win rate.
31. Banking habit was checked head-to-head (600 games per policy): waiting to bank was no better than banking at once within noise, so bots that stand in for people bank promptly.

## Removed with the original rule set (for the record)
Movement (0–3 spaces on a hex board, going around or pushing other pieces), the six corner research sites, die-rolled delivery assignments with repeat deposits, "50% cut" delivery of one fossil per set, and the rulebook's gem order. The decisions behind them are in the git history before 2026-10-18.

## Process
32. Tests use Node's built-in runner (`npm test`); the browser tests use Playwright if installed and skip themselves otherwise. A build runs the tests first, so a failing test keeps the old version live.
64. **Home board reverted to the ambient flip (playtest 7):** the owner preferred the original title-screen picture — the real board with a few random tiles flipping face up and back on a slow loop (CSS only, one peek ring) — over the scripted demo game, so items 44 and 54 no longer apply. The one-screen layout from playtest 4 stays. It holds still under reduced motion.
65. **Quieter table, shorter menu (playtest 7):** the concealed/public colour key above the board is gone (the tiles say it themselves). The in-game menu is Retry, How to play, Settings and **Main Menu** (in that order) (was "Back to the start"); there is no Resume — tapping away from the menu closes it. Supersedes the button list in item 38.
66. **No bonus die (playtest 7):** banking scores the number on the set's fossil, once. The d6 chain (+1 per 6, rolling again) is removed from the rules, the data block (`bonusDieSides/Face/Points`), the engine, the bots' equity model and every screen (the bank banner shows just "+n"). Supersedes the bonus in items 12, 27, 37 and 42. The die is still used for who goes first. Saved games made before this change are cleared (the rules fingerprint changed).
67. **Focus is removed; the levels are retuned (playtest 7).** The opponent no longer plays an unexplained random move; it is weaker only through a fading Memory (a wrong guess still taps a neighbouring or random other spot) and lower senses. Memory sits high from Normal up — the owner saw Normal miss matches it had just seen — so Normal is "pretty good at memory, suboptimal elsewhere": levels are Memory 4.8 / 7.4 / 8.6 / 9.2 / 9.8 with senses 0 / 3 / 5 / 7–8 / 9–10. Each level beats the one below about 71–78% of the time over 500 games per pair. Replaces the level numbers in item 61 and the Focus skill in items 55–63.
68. **A slip is smarter and teaches the bot (playtest 7).** Beginner repeated the same wrong match three turns running: its memory of the second fossil failed, it tapped a random neighbour (even one it already knew was a 2), and still believed the same thing next turn. Now a slip taps a spot it does not know holds a *different* fossil (unknown spots first), and the failed belief is dropped, so it re-plans. Still a near-miss half the time, still random otherwise.
69. **Memory is the primary stat; the rest are minor behaviours (owner, playtest 7).** The levels differ mostly in Memory (5.2 / 7.0 / 8.5 / 9.3 / 9.85); the senses add small, visible differences in play. Normal is "pretty good at memory, suboptimal in other skills".
70. **Tapping your own peeked fossil does nothing** (it used to hide the number again). **Win screen** border is the winner's own colour.
71. **Not yet a skill — reading what the opponent knows.** The idea of making the public (second) flip a fossil the opponent has already seen (e.g. one it peeked at, shown by its dashed ring) so that only you learn something is not implemented: it would need a new skill (working name *Table reading*) and a way to track what each opponent has seen. My earlier 'flip only already-public fossils' trial lost, but this version is different, because only the flipper learns anything. Recorded in `docs/AI.md`.
72. **Sound (playtest 7).** Synthesised with Web Audio from recipes in the data block (`GAME_DATA.sound`), so the game stays one offline file. Principles: on by default but switchable in Settings (not in the ☰ menu itself) and remembered; audio is created/resumed only inside a tap (iPad Safari rule); short soft sounds behind one master level and a limiter; the same sound is not retriggered faster than 40 ms and voices are capped; it stops when the page is hidden; the device silent switch still applies. Each game moment has one sound (peek, flip + outcome, match, gem, bank, slip, win, lose, unlock). Sounds for an opponent's moves play as each move is shown, not when it was decided; moves skipped by a tap, or under the reduced-motion setting, are silent. The recipes cannot be heard by the test suite — they are checked for gentleness (length, pitch range, loudness) and for being triggered; how they *sound* is for the owner's ears.
73. **Reset campaign (playtest 7):** Settings always offers it; it asks first, then forgets every win and the unlock-all switch. Replaces 'Lock them again', which only appeared after unlock-all.
74. **Where to check how many of each fossil there are (playtest 7):** not in How to play (the counts may differ by level) but in a **Fossils** entry in the ☰ menu, after How to play. It lists each number and the gems with its count, pairs/sets, and the total, taken from the running game's own data (`state.data.tokens`), so a future level with different counts shows its own. It never shows what remains or has been seen. How to play just points to it.
75. **A gem always carries its worth (playtest 7).** A gem has no number of its own, so wherever it appears with a value to act on it shows 💎 with the worth (12, read from the data block) underneath: on its fossil once face up or peeked, on a held set (so you see what a bank scores), and in the end-screen rows. The Fossils panel gives the worth in words. The home-screen decoration is left as it was.
76. **Peek means flip; tap anywhere to end (playtest 7).** A turn is peek + flip, or bank (only at the start of the turn). After a peek the only legal move is to flip another fossil — no End turn and no bank. That also closes a loophole (peek, learn, then bank for free). End turn is offered only once you have flipped (or are in the gem-third step); then a tap anywhere — on a fossil, empty space or the End turn button — ends the turn, except that with two gems showing, a tap on a third fossil is the gem consent. Tapping your own peeked fossil before flipping does nothing.
77. **Library boxes (playtest 7):** the owner found the library page flat next to Fossil Frenzy's toy-like home screen, so each game is now a board-game box: cover art that shows what you get to play with, the title on the art, and an info band (players, time). Art is drawn by a small function per game in the library page, chosen by the catalogue's `art` id, so every game looks different. The way back to the library from a game is a prominent chip in the very top-left of its home screen.
78. **How to play is pictures first (playtest 7):** peek, flip, match and bank are four small illustrated cards; gems and winning get one line each; the judgment calls (kept because every call must be listed in-game) are folded under "The fine print". Counts of each number are not in it — they differ by level — see the Fossils panel (item 74).
79. **Speed review (playtest 7):** measured, the game is not slow: a redraw takes about a millisecond and the strongest opponent decides in about 0.04 ms (worst 3 ms) over 4,800 decisions. The one real time sink was the test suite (a test waiting 30 s for a button that never appears), now fixed; the browser tests are the rest of the ~2 minutes and are mostly full games played by taps.


## Levels with their own boards (owner, 2026-10-24)
64. **Each campaign level has its own board and fossils** (data block: `shapes` and each level's `shape` / `tokens`): Beginner is a 18-space **donut** (2×4, 3×4, 5×4, 6×4, 9×2, no gems); Novice a 21-space **triangle** (the same fossils plus 3 gems worth 10); Normal a 25-space **sun** (2×6, 3×4, 5×4, 6×4, 9×2, 10×2, gems 11); Challenging the 31-space **flower** (2×6, 3×6, 5×4, 6×4, 7×4, 9×2, 10×2, gems 12); Master the 37-space **giant hexagon** (2×8, 3×8, 5×4, 6×4, 7×4, 9×2, 10×2, 11×2, gems 15). The shapes are in cube coordinates (x + y + z = 0): the engine reads `q = x`, `r = z`. The opponents' skills did not change.
65. **Pass & Play stays on the flower with the 31 fossils** (it is the same board and set as the Challenging level), so nothing about Pass & Play changed. "Fossils in this game" and the help text follow the game on the table (no gem line on level 1, where there are no gems).
66. **Novice (level 2) opens with an alert introducing the gems** — what a gem is worth and how a set of three is made — every time that level starts; one tap puts it away (no timer). The wording is in the level's `alert` in the data block.
67. A saved campaign game is resumed on its own level's board and fossils (the save's fingerprint includes the shape).

## Torn Treasure, portrait first, saved games, reminders (owner, 2026-10-24)
80. **Torn Treasure is a re-theme only.** Rules, scoring and numbers are untouched; the folder, the engine's names (`bank`, `sets`) and the storage keys stay `fossil-frenzy` (no broken links or lost saves). What the player reads is treasure-map wording: tile (a face-down fossil), map (a matched set), found / dug up (held / banked), gem map with three pieces. "Bank" is "dig up".
81. **Face up, a tile is half a map in one of four tiers** (data: `theme.tiers`): 2–3 plain, 5–7 aged with a compass rose, 9+ gold with an ornate border (11 where used is gold too), gems dark. The torn side is chosen by the tile's number so it is stable; each of a gem map's three pieces has a different torn section. A gem still shows its worth (small, under the jewel) — the earlier rule that a gem always carries what it is worth wins over "a jewel instead of a number", because the worth is what you act on.
82. **A match ends the turn by itself** once its film has played (the owner asked for no waiting on a tap). A tap skips the film. With reduced motion there is no film and the old "tap anywhere to end your turn" stays. The film's timings are in the data block (`anim`).
83. **Digging up also plays a film** (map to open chest, points count up) and then ends the turn. A tap skips it. The count-up is presentation only: the engine already has the points.
84. **First-match reminder only on the easiest level, once per device** (the owner's words: "the first time you make a match" on the easiest difficulty). Gem reminders are on every level that has gems, once per device. In Pass & Play every reminder is once per game and "Rules reminders" (setup screen, default on, remembered) switches them off. A reminder waits for its "Got it" (no timer); the first-match one then ends the turn.
85. **Pass & Play shows what the others flipped since your last turn:** every fossil flipped publicly (the one flipped second; with three gems, the third and the revealed peek too) and still on the board, face up with a ring in the flipper's colour, from the moment you tap "Tap to start your turn" until your turn ends. Never the fossil only peeked. It is a memory aid for the person who was not looking at the device; it is not used in the Campaign, where the opponent's turn is shown to you.
86. **Leaving a game keeps it** (Menu → Main Menu used to discard it). The save carries `left`: a reload does not drop you back into a game you left. Continue is on the Main Menu and on the Campaign / Pass & Play screen it belongs to; starting a new game replaces it. A finished game is not kept.
87. **Portrait first** (iPad 820 × 1180, then iPhone 17 402 × 874); landscape and other sizes must still work. Buttons: menu 68 px (80 on iPad), End turn and "Tap to start your turn" bigger than before, the back button 48 px, level cards at least 96 px tall.
88. **A tile is torn only when it is matched** (owner): a flipped tile that does not match is a plain hexagon; a matched tile is torn on the side facing the rest of its set (the film fades the plain tile into the torn one as it lifts), the two sides of a pair mirror each other so they fit. Gem pieces differ by their drawing, not by a torn edge, until they match.
89. **A film's maps are not drawn back on the board** (owner, about the opponent). After the film the cells they left stay empty, with a faint dashed outline in the finder's colour until the turn ends, so you can see where they were. With reduced motion (no film) a match stays face up until the turn is over, as before.
90. **Screens are centred, not top-aligned** (owner): every screen but the front page centres its content on the page; a screen taller than the page scrolls from its top. The end screen always offers the same level again (campaign) or the same players (Pass & Play).
91. **Pass & Play board sizes (owner):** the five boards of the campaign levels (data `passBoards`; Tiny 18, Small 21, Medium 25, Large 31 = the old Pass & Play board, Huge 37), each with its own tiles and gems. The default follows the number of players (`passBoardByPlayers`: 2 Medium, 3 Large, 4 Huge — more people, more tiles, so everyone still gets turns); a size somebody picks stays until they pick another, whatever the player count. The game, its replay and its save keep the board they started on. A Pass & Play game saved before sizes existed resumes on the 31-tile board.
92. **The game opens on the Main Menu** (owner), whatever was being played; a saved game is Continue (Main Menu, Campaign, Pass & Play screens). A reload therefore never drops you back in mid-turn; Continue puts a Pass & Play game behind its turn card.
93. **Gems are explained when a gem is first revealed, not at the start of level 2** (owner; replaces decision 66). One reminder, `coach.gem`, covers what a gem map is, what it is worth, and the third tap; there is no second one for two gems. It fires on a gem I peek or flip, on one the opponent flips (its turn waits for the tap), and on one the previous Pass & Play player showed. The note is placed on the half of the page the gem is not in, and the gem tile is redrawn above the dimmed page, so it is never hidden.
94. **Two saved games, asked about when you choose a mode (owner):** the most recent Campaign game and the most recent Pass & Play game are each kept (own keys). The Main Menu has no Continue button: Campaign and Pass & Play ask "Continue this game / Start a new game (replaces it)" only when a game of that kind is saved. Starting a new game of one kind never touches the other. A finished game is not kept. A save from before this (one slot) moves to the slot of its kind.
95. **The end of the game waits for a tap (owner):** the last match plays its film (the same as any match); then the board stays and a tap anywhere (or "See the results") brings up the results. Nothing on a timer. The campaign result is recorded when the game ends, not when the screen is shown.
96. **The look of Torn Treasure (owner asked for a fresh pass):** one palette — dark leather (`--bg`), parchment (`--parch`) for what you read and tap, brown ink, red wax for danger and the X, gold leaf for the main action. Serif lettering for titles, names and numbers; the system sans stays for small print. The tiles keep their sand and map art; ink colours by tier are unchanged. The box art in the library is the same story as the game: a torn map and sand tiles.
