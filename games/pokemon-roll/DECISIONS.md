# Pokémon Roll! — decisions

Sources (Patrick's Google Drive, folder "Pokémon Roll"): **Pokémon Roll! Rulebook**, **Pokémon Roll design doc**, **Pokémon Roll cards** (sheet, tab `v2`, 175 cards). The rulebook is a draft full of open questions; every call below is mine unless marked *(owner)*.

## Decided by the owner
- *(owner, later)* **One reroll per round, per side** ("add back in that you can reroll once"): on top of any "+1 reroll" a card gives. Easy never rerolls and hands dice out at random.
- *(owner)* **One roll.** Each side rolls one die per card; the strategy is which die goes on which card. Cards that say "+1 reroll" or "you may always reroll 3s" still do, because the card says so.
- *(owner)* **Solo against the computer** (Easy / Normal / Master); no pass and play for now.
- *(owner)* **Legendaries are in.** The nine level-6 cards that are not starters: Articuno, Zapdos, Moltres, Dragonite, Mewtwo, Suicune, Lugia, Ho-oh, Celebi. Once you have two dice, a 9 on two dice at the start of a turn spots one: it is shuffled into the place on its card (with that place's discard pile). Lance holds one, drawn at the start of the game.
- *(owner)* **A blocker takes the leftover.** If a block cannot soak the whole attack, the blocker takes what is left.
- *(owner)* **A card is out the moment it reaches 0 health.**
- *(owner)* **Bosses get a random team** from the places on their boss card (Regular or Extra Long game).
- *(owner)* **Art:** Crystal sprites, shown as big as the card allows. A card drawn from the wild has a fresh **10% chance to be shiny** each time; a card on your team or on a boss's team keeps its form for good. Shiny is **purely cosmetic**, with a sparkle when it comes into play.
- *(owner)* **Card design:** the weakness is a tab flush with the top of the card.

- *(owner)* **No opening rival fight.** In the paper game it only decides who goes first, so it is gone. `RULING:` your partner starts at level 2 (the level that fight gave), and Viridian Forest, Mt. Moon and Brock are open from the start. There is no rival at all in solo: beating Lance ends the game.
- *(owner)* **One card design, always whole.** The same card (name, level, HP, weakness tab, sprite, element, role, ability, all six faces, special move) is used on the table, in your hand, in the picker and in your collection. There is no tap-to-enlarge version. The row for the die you pick lights up on every card, and the row a card is using this round is lit. Cards are as big as the table allows (at least 150 wide); a very wide team scrolls sideways.
- *(owner)* **Tap anywhere to roll** after a round; there is no Next button. The dice tumble when they are rolled. There is no Suggest button.
- *(owner)* **The map** runs left to right, each place drawn as a little scene. It shows what is open and one step beyond it (so the two paths after Brock show as a fork until they meet at Blue).

- *(owner)* **No team to pick; your whole collection is your hand.** For a wild encounter a wild card comes up and the computer puts it down; you put one of yours down; then you may fight it one on one, or bring another (up to your dice), and each time they draw one more wild card, which the computer places. You never decide a number up front. For a gym leader the computer puts down one card, then you and they take turns (two at a time), and you put down as many as they bring (or as many as you have).
- *(owner)* **A gym leader's team is a surprise.** Their team is drawn from the places on their boss card before the game begins and is kept face down until they play it; nothing shows it on the map or before the fight. The eye on a gym leader on the map explains how the team was made (and from which places). If a fight goes badly you can leave at any time.
- *(owner)* **Only choose when there is a choice.** A lone card with a lone spot goes down by itself; a lone card with several spots is already picked; a lone die goes onto a lone card; and a true one-on-one with no reroll to think about plays out by itself.
- *(owner)* **A round plays out one card at a time**, in order (healing first, then left to right with your card first in each column). Each card's turn shows an arrow from the attacker to its target, the damage number, HP going from this to that, and a one-line picture with the two sprites. You tap to see the next card (Skip plays the rest). An "or" (attack this card or that one) is asked when that card's turn comes, not when the die is given.
- *(owner)* **Words on the cards:** moves read "3 Attack", "2 Block", "4 Heal", "1 Damage 💥" (damage the card does to itself). A shield ("-1d" on the sheet) soaks that much damage **once a round**, of any kind, including damage a card does to itself (so Metapod's 2 Damage 💥 becomes 1).
- *(owner)* **Their cards are upside down**, head to head across the middle, like a player sitting opposite you.
- *(owner)* **Dice:** drag a die onto a card (or tap the die, then the card); it lands in the card's art area. Tap anywhere to roll the next round; the dice tumble. Sprites are cropped so each Pokémon fills its art box.
- *(owner)* **Menus:** the menu holds How to Play (pictures, not paragraphs), Settings (Tips, Computer difficulty), Give up, and Save & main menu. New Game asks for a second tap when it would delete a saved game. In Your cards, a tap fills the screen with that card.

## Reading the sheet
- `RULING:` **Move 1–6 are the six faces of the die.** A card with fewer than six moves has blank faces (a blank is a die with nothing to do). The sheet lists the moves strongest first, and the design doc says blanks exist.
- `RULING:` **Arrows.** `<` the column to the left, `^` the card facing it, `>` the column to the right, `v` itself, `<<` two columns. `<^>` is all of them at once; `<^ or >` is a choice of one of them; `and/or` takes everything that is there. `<<<<^ or ^>>>>` is one side or the other. For an attack `^` is the matchup; for a heal or block `v` is the card itself and `<`/`>` are its neighbours.
- `RULING:` **`over`** (`7a over <^>`) splits the points among the targets however you like; the game gives each point to wherever it does the most good. **`, +2a <>`** is an extra attack on the neighbours that happens only if the card attacked. **`or` between two moves** (`3a <^> or 3h <v>`) is a choice of one; the game takes the one worth more right now. **`&` and `,`** do both.
- `RULING:` A block's arrows say **who it can protect**. It stays up the whole round and soaks attacks as they come, from itself first, then (if it helps) one neighbour per attack.
- `RULING:` **Special moves** (`66: 12a`) need that many dice on that number on the same card; those dice are all it gets, so some other card goes without a die that round.
- `RULING:` Weakness is the card's weak element against the element of the attacking card. Weak types the sheet gives that are not elements ("Enemy Heals", "Ghost") mean no weakness.
- `RULING:` A **blocker that is itself weak** to the attack takes the doubled amount before the block soaks it (rulebook).

## The fight
- Cards stand in columns facing one opponent. The computer puts down one card, then both sides two at a time; you may go first instead if you tap your own card first. Columns never exceed the team size.
- Each round: roll → give dice → **heal first**, then cards act from left to right, **your card first in each column** (cards that "attack first" go before everyone). Damage is double on a weakness; a card at 0 is out at once and does not act later in the round.
- After a round, knocked-out cards leave, empty columns close, and cards left without a matchup are paired with a matchless card across the table next to them (the side with fewer cards moves). `RULING:` the rulebook's turn-by-turn choosing is automatic.
- `RULING:` If both sides fall in the same round: **sudden death**. Each side keeps its best card that was standing at the start of the round (at that health); the side that deals more damage in the next round wins; a tie repeats up to three times, then the computer wins.
- `RULING:` A fight with no damage or healing for **six rounds** is lost (the rulebook says a stalemate is a loss for the hero), and so is one that lasts 60 rounds. "Give up" is in the menu.
- Your cards are never lost in a fight. A fight is lost, nothing happens.

## The campaign (the design doc's Map 2)
- You start with **one die** (one card per challenge). Each gym leader you beat is another die (up to six). Challenge a place with as many cards as you have dice; it brings the same number. Win and you keep one of theirs (the rest go to the place's discard pile); lose and they go back to the deck.
- Gym leaders: Brock 2, Misty 3, Lt. Surge 3, Blaine 4/5, Erika 4/5, Blue 5/7, Lance 6/9 (Regular/Extra Long), drawn from the places on the boss card. `RULING:` you take as many cards as you have (up to the boss's number): an outnumbered fight is allowed, as the rulebook says.
- `RULING:` The map opens like the design doc's list: Viridian Forest, Mt. Moon, Brock → Power Plant, Misty, Surge → S.S. Anne / Pokemon Tower, Blaine / Erika → Safari Zone, Cinnabar Island, Blue (needs both Blaine and Erika) → Seafoam, Victory Road, Lance (the last fight; there is no rival in solo).
- Your partner starts at level 2 and levels to one above the level on the boss's card when you beat them. Pikachu may evolve into Raichu at level 5 (you choose once; Raichu then goes on to level 6).
- When a place's deck runs out it is refilled from its discard pile. After beating a gym leader, their team goes to the discard piles of the places it came from.
- Beating Lance gives you his legendary.

## Abilities
Every ability on the sheet is read from its text by the table in the data block. **Not in this version:** Jynx's "On KO, freeze an ally die" (Jynx's freeze is still unclear). A test lists them so nothing is silently dropped.
- `RULING:` "Loses a round" (and "freeze") means that card gets no die next round. "Cannot attack next round" is the same.
- `RULING:` "Attackers take 1d" hurts the attacker whenever it damages the card; "When KO'd, attacker takes 4d" is plain damage that nothing blocks.
- `RULING:` Auras ("+1a <>") add to a neighbour's matching moves; they do not add to the card's own moves. "Neighbor moves +<" adds 1 to the card on its left, "+>" to the card on its right.
- `RULING:` Dice-changing abilities (Golduck, Exeggcute, Exeggutor, Staryu) are a button on the card sheet ("Use ability on its die") during the dice step.
- `RULING:` "Dodge" means the attack does nothing; "immune" cards take no damage from that element; "absorb" (Oddish vs water, Magmar vs fire) heals instead.
- `RULING:` The heal ability "Receives double effect from healing" doubles heals on that card only.

- *(owner)* **Magikarp:** while it has allies, anything the card directly across from it attacks with is cut by 9. Alone, it is an ordinary card.
- `RULING:` **Slowpoke/Slowbro** lower the die of the card facing them by 1 (1 becomes 6), every other round, automatically. **Porygon** (every other round) gives a teammate's attack the element its target is weak to. **Weezing** hands one of its damage to a healthier card facing it at the end of the round. **Dratini** lets its dragon allies' plain attacks hit left, across or right. **Lugia** takes one reroll from the other side.

## Not in this version
- Colored dice and the elemental dice rules, trading cards, the GM-swapping two-player game, Map 1 (the same data would do it), the ten-card hard-mode rival, quests, and the Hall of Fame.

## Computer levels (owner: "build the best AI you can and call it Master; Normal is between perfect and random; Easy may be completely random")
- **Master** = the best play we have: exhaustive-ish dice-to-card search with pair swaps, special moves weighed against ordinary ones, matchup-based placement, and a reroll whenever the average plan after a fresh throw beats the current plan by a margin. **Normal** = Master's play, but it never rerolls (owner's call: half-random placements didn't feel like Normal). **Easy** = everything random, never rerolls. Measured by playing the Master hero bot against each (10 campaigns): the hero loses 6% of battles to Easy, 25% to Normal, 34% to Master.
- Saved settings `medium`/`hard` are read as Normal/Master.
- **Records, shiny per game (10%, starter keeps its form), draw pile used before dug cards are reshuffled, team sorted by level then oldest, dice are never given out for you:** see the changelog.

## Moving cards and Haunter (owner: "build the movement abilities"; Haunter: "when one of your cards is KO'd you can choose to retain its ability, once per battle")
- `RULING:` **Kadabra** ("after each round") and **Celebi** ("before every roll phase") are the same thing here: once a round, before you give out dice, a ◀ / ▶ on the card swaps it with the card in the next column on its side (or slides into an empty spot). **Abra** swaps two of their cards the same way (tap ⇄, then tap two of theirs). Left and right are the screen's.
- `RULING:` **Machop / Machoke / Machamp**: a Fighting attack pushes a card it hits one column the way the attack reached (a `<` hit pushes left, a `>` hit right; a straight hit pushes nothing). **Rhyhorn / Rhydon**: a Ground attack slides every card on the other side one column left / right, into any gap. A card cannot be pushed past the edge.
- `RULING:` **Golbat / Tangela**: cards on the other side cannot move or push anything of anyone's (their pushes and swaps do nothing).
- `RULING:` **Alakazam**: once a round, an attack aimed at one of its team that would hurt more than it would on another ally goes to that ally instead ("ally" is your own card, per the rulebook's glossary). It is automatic, and the card shows what happened.
- `RULING:` **Haunter**: when a teammate is KO'd while Haunter is still up, you are asked to keep that card's ability(ies) on Haunter or say no thanks; using it once spends it for the battle. The computer always keeps the first one. Haunter keeps it only as long as Haunter is up.
- The computer (Normal and Master) moves cards when it makes its dice worth more; Easy never moves them.

## Falling at 0 (owner: "all Pokémon get to do their moves, so they are KO'd at the end of the round — but a Pokémon at 0 cannot be healed")
- `RULING:` Replaces the earlier "KO'd the moment it hits 0". A card at 0 or below stays on the table for the rest of the round: it still acts, still blocks, and can still be hit (it is just worth nothing to aim at). It cannot be healed. When the round ends every card at 0 falls together, and "when KO'd" abilities (attacker loses a round, thorns, Jynx-style effects, Haunter) trigger then, against whoever last hit it. A card taken to 0 by a burn when cards enter a column falls before the next round starts.
- The computer values a card at 0 as a worthless target and never heals it.

## Rerolls, decisions and the card (owner messages of the last session)
- *(owner)* **Reroll:** every round you may give out any dice you like, then reroll the rest together, once; rerolled dice are locked in. You and Master get it (cards that say "+1 reroll" add more such rerolls); Normal and Easy never reroll. A card that says you may always reroll a face still does that one die, free. `RULING:` the computer decides which of its dice to throw before it gives any out.
- *(owner)* **Decisions are yours.** Anything a card lets you choose stops the round and asks you. Built: **dodges** (Pikachu "Dodge one attack per match", "dodge the first attack against you this round", "each round dodge all attacks after the first": asked every time one of your cards is attacked, Dodge! or Take it; declining keeps the dodge for later), "or" targets, Haunter, Abra, Kadabra, Celebi, ability dice (Staryu, Porygon, Slowpoke use buttons). `RULING:` the computer always uses a dodge. **Still automatic (to build next):** "hangs on" survival, Ditto's choice of whose move to use, and blocking (blocks apply to any attack they cover).
- `RULING:` The weakness is a full-width strip across the top of the card in the weakness's colour; art is 44% of the card width.

## Blocks and Ditto (owner: "you choose who to block, you can only be in one place at a time"; "build Ditto"; "hang on survival is not a choice")
- `RULING:` A block (any block that can reach more than one card) protects exactly one card: the blocker or a neighbour the block can reach. You pick it on the blocker's turn (the round's attacks are already on show). The computer protects the card facing the biggest hit. A block that can only reach one card just applies.
- `RULING:` **Ditto** ("May use moves on matchup's or neighbors' cards"): read as it uses the move printed on the card across from it, or on one of that card's neighbours (the opposing cards one column either side); you choose at the start of the round's resolution. If you are the computer or only one card is there it picks the best move. The sheet does not say whether "neighbors" means theirs or its own side, so this reading is open to change.
- **Survival ("hangs on")** is automatic, never a question.

## Coloured dice (owner: "matching die colour to a move counts as +1 to that move; remove the doubling of specials")
- *(owner)* **A die the colour of a move's element adds +1 to that move.** No doubling for specials.
- `RULING:` Where the colours come from (rulebook + design doc, Map 2): Brock brown = Ground, Misty blue = Water, Lt. Surge yellow = Electric, Erika green = Grass, Blaine red = Fire; Blue gives none; Lance uses one of each. Beating a leader (not Lance) keeps their colour for the rest of the game. In a gym fight both sides use the leader's colour (you even if you do not own it yet); wild fights are white for the computer. First dice are white (the starter does not give a colour; the paper game only uses coloured dice to pick starters). A coloured die takes the place of a white die (you still roll one die per card), so the first dice of your roll are the coloured ones. The +1 goes on the move's main effect (not on a "+2" bonus part). Gyarados's "+1a if given the blue die" is the same idea with a fixed colour.

## Which die rolls which move (owner: "move 1 is triggered on a 6 ... damage to self is always on a 1")
- *(owner)* The sheet's move 1 is rolled on a **6**, move 2 on a 5, move 3 on a 4 and so on. A move that only hurts the card itself is always at the bottom: the worst on a 1, the second worst on a 2; the numbers between are blank. (Pikachu: 3 Attack on 6, 2 Attack on 5, 2 Heal on 4, 2 Attack on 3, nothing on 2, 1 Damage on 1.) Cards are shown 6 at the top, 1 at the bottom. A few backwards cards (1 best, 6 worst) can come later.
- `RULING:` A move that both attacks and hurts the card (Voltorb's "4 Attack, 4 Damage") is an ordinary move, not a self-damage move, so it keeps its place from the top. `rules.moveOrder` in the data block switches back to the old order (`'sheet'`) for tests and experiments.
- *(owner)* **Special moves keep their dice numbers** (Fire 6s, Grass 5s, Ground 4s, Electric 3s, Water 2s) for now.

## Shinies (owner, later: "make shinies have one more health than usual")
- *(owner)* A shiny card has **+1 health** (`rules.shinyHp`), on top of looking different and sparkling when it is played. This replaces "purely cosmetic".
