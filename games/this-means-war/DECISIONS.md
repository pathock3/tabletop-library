# This Means WAR! — decisions

The newest rulebook ("Quickfire Kingdoms" inside) is the source. Not built yet: tunnels and the hero (special ability) cards.

- Card names: Leader / General / Warrior / Ranger / Sorcerer / Spy / Assassin (the 2022 doc says Queen/Knight/Archer, speed 1, 3 starting castles; not used).
- "Attacking card" in a weakness comparison = the higher-power card; if the lower card is its weakness, the higher card is defeated and the lower survives.
- Equal power: both cards defeated.
- Both sides running out of cards at once: nobody wins, the castle stays as it was, both armies are gone.
- Several battles in one turn: resolved in board order (the attacker's choice of order is not offered yet).
- The opponent sees how many cards are in each unit, never which. The defender's card is face up before the attacker chooses.
- Dealing a card to a castle that already holds a unit lacking that type joins that unit; otherwise it makes a new unit there (merged, duplicates discarded, at the Battle step).
- Capturing the castle holding the enemy flag wins, including by walking onto it undefended.
- Pass and Play uses a cover screen between turns and between battle choices.
- A game that nobody can finish ends as a draw after 300 turns.
- Factions (from the owner's cards sheet): a faction gives each of the seven roles a character, with the same role, power and weakness; "+1 P" / "-1 P" on the sheet changes that card's power (Sauron 15, Gollum 3, Aragorn 11, Legolas 9, Gandalf the Grey 7, Tyrion 11, Jon Snow 9, Varys 5, Russell Hantz 3). A faction is offered only when all seven roles have a special ability or a changed power (the owner: a faction with names but no abilities is not complete).
- Special abilities are stored in the data block. **Played so far: five Survivor abilities** (below); everything else (all the other factions, Survivor's Spy Shack and Cause Chaos, allies, Gandalf the White's flip) is written on the cards but not played yet.
- Offered: Basic, The Fellowship, Mordor, The Benders, Game of Thrones, Survivor, Pokémon. Not offered: Order of the Phoenix (needs General, Warrior, Spy), Death Eaters, Rebel Alliance, Galactic Empire, Pixar and Disney Heroes and Villains, Studio Ghibli.
- Choosing: a universe pair (only Fellowship/Mordor is complete so far) gives the computer the other side; any single faction gives it a random different one. You are Blue against the computer; in Pass and Play Red picks, then Blue.

## Abilities, battles and screens (v3)
- **An ability is used on purpose.** Tapping a card in a battle only chooses it; the Play button plays it. At the start of a battle there is a panel listing the abilities you can use (with the card and its rule), then a Start fighting button; a player with nothing to use skips it. Cards with an ability you can use are ringed.
- `RULING:` Outwit (Yul): only the attacker, at the start of a battle. The defender's first card of the battle is played face down; the attacker guesses its role. Right: that card is defeated and they play another. Wrong: it stays face down for the attacker until both have played.
- `RULING:` Idol Play (Parvati): while Parvati is in your battle army (hand or set aside) and you must play first, you may put an idol card down with your card: the Idol or No Idol. After the other player plays it is revealed; if it is the Idol your card is safe this round. The Idol can be used once per battle; No Idol (a bluff) any number of times. The two idol cards are shown on the screen when it applies.
- `RULING:` Blindsided (Rob): at the start of a battle, move Rob from another of your units into your battle hand (once; not if you already have a Leader there).
- `RULING:` Flip Allegiances (Cirie): when you are attacked, at the start of the battle, steal a random card from your opponent's battle hand; if you already hold that role, the stolen card is discarded.
- `RULING:` Anyone But Me (Sandra): when you played Sandra first (she is exposed), after the other player plays you may swap her for a card in your hand, or keep her.
- Not played: Spy Shack (Tony) and Cause Chaos (Russell) (they are not battle abilities and need rules for structures and a d6 table), and every other faction's abilities.
- Battles: a battle no longer starts the moment Resolve is pressed. The board shows a clash marker at the castle and a Start battle button; when a battle ends a banner says who won (and who captures or holds the castle) before the next one. A round shows both cards and marks the winner (SURVIVES / SAFE) and the loser (DEFEATED). The computer's deploys and moves are slower and units slide to where they go.
- Resolve asks first ("tap again") when you still have units that can move. Tapping a selected unit again cancels. Any of your units can be opened at any time (the cards inside show), and the menu has Your units and Your faction's cards. Choosing a side has a "See all 7 cards" button on every faction. The button back to the Tabletop Library is only on the main menu.

## Boards, tunnels, supply, retreat and permanent losses (v4)
- **Boards are data** (`maps` in the data block): `id`, `name`, `description`, `hexes` (axial `q, r` pairs: q the column, r the row), `castles` (`id`, `name`, `hex`, `startingOwner` red/blue/null) and an `orientation`. Adding a board is only data. The screen offers **Small** (recommended for learning) and **Large**; both were drawn by Patrick and are flat-top. **Small**: 7 castles, three each side and one in the middle, on a hexagon of 19 spaces (the empty spaces around its edge were removed). **Large**: 10 castles, three each and four between. `RULING:` the shapes come from reading his two pictures: the castle positions are exact, the edge spaces are my best reading, so check them. The spec's provisional `large-provisional` map was not needed once the real boards arrived. `RULING:` Patrick's boards give each side **three** starting castles (the old rules had two), so a flag hides in one of three. The original board (two a side) stays in the data as `classic`, not offered on the screen; the tests use it.
- **Tunnels** are worked out from castle positions (two castles 2 or fewer apart, `rules.tunnelRange`), never stored. A unit on a castle its side holds may use its one action to move to any other castle in the same chain of castles that side holds. Tunnels are drawn as dotted lines, gold where you hold both ends; selecting a unit rings the castles it can reach by tunnel.
- **Supply**: you can deploy only at a starting castle you still hold, or any castle in a chain with one. Other held castles are marked "cut off" and dimmed; the deploy highlight shows only castles in supply.
- **Retreat**: when it is your turn to play and you have exactly one card in the battle (one in hand, none set aside) you may retreat it to a neighbouring space with no enemy unit, never onto a castle you do not hold, and never a space where another fight is waiting. You lose the battle, the other side stays and captures a castle as usual (and wins if it held the flag). `RULING:` a card the other side had already put forward this round survives. The card moves as a one-card unit (merging with an allied unit there). The message does not name the card. The computer never retreats.
- **Permanent losses**: a defeated card goes to the Fallen pile (face up, tap it to see it). The deck is never reshuffled; at the end of your turn you draw 2, or what is left. You may keep cards in hand. The hand limit is 7: after drawing you choose what to lose to the Fallen pile. A merge duplicate goes to the bottom of its owner's draw pile. A player with no units, no hand and no deck loses. `RULING:` a card lost to Cirie's steal (a duplicate role) also goes to the Fallen pile.
- **Abilities that mention the discard pile** now say Fallen pile (text only: none of them is played yet): Sauron (The Dark Lord Rises), Luke (Rebels spy), Tacullu (Counter Summon), Breaker/Memory Break (send to the Fallen pile instead of discard), the Benders effect "place one in their Fallen pile", and the Game of Thrones card that moves a card from the Fallen pile to the top of the deck. No implemented ability returns a card from the Fallen pile, so that part of the spec is not testable yet.
- Already matching (left alone): the defender plays first in the first exchange and the lead then alternates; a lower card is defeated unless it is the weakness; a tie defeats both; surviving cards form a new hand; units hold 1 to 7 cards with no duplicate type; each turn ends by drawing 2.
- Saved games from before this update cannot be continued.
